<p><i><b>Alliaria petiolata</b></i>, or <b>garlic mustard</b>, is a biennial flowering plant in the mustard family (Brassicaceae). It is native to Europe, western and central Asia, north-western Africa, Morocco, Iberia and the British Isles, north to northern Scandinavia, and east to northern Pakistan and Xinjiang in western China. <h1 class="title"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymantria_dispar">Spongy Moth</a></h1>
<div class="subheader"><em>Lymantria dispar</em> · Insecta · 1 observation</div>
<div class="description">Species of moth</div>
<p><i><b>Lymantria dispar</b></i>, also known as the <b>gypsy moth</b>, and in the United States, the <b>spongy moth</b>, is a species of moth in the family Erebidae native to Europe and Asia. <i>Lymantria dispar</i> is subdivided into several subspecies, with subspecies such as <i>L. d. dispar</i> and <i>L. d. japonica</i> being clearly identifiable without ambiguity. <i>Lymantria dispar</i> has been introduced to several continents and is now additionally found as an invasive species in Africa, North America and South America. The polyphagous larvae live on a variety of deciduous and coniferous trees and can cause severe damage in years of mass reproduction. Due to these features, <i>Lymantria dispar</i> is listed among the world's 100 worst invasive alien species.</p>

<h1 class="title"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphimallon_solstitiale">Summer Chafer</a></h1>
<div class="subheader"><em>Amphimallon solstitiale</em> · Insecta · 1 observation</div>
<div class="description">Species of beetle</div>
<p><i><b>Amphimallon solstitiale</b></i>, also known as the <b>summer chafer</b> or <b>European june beetle</b>, is a beetle similar to the cockchafer but much smaller, approximately 20 millimetres (0.79 in) in length. They are declining in numbers now, but where found they are often seen in large numbers. At dusk they actively fly around tree tops looking for a mate and can often be found drowning in pools of water the following morning. They are also attracted to light and come in through open, lit windows and fly around lamps, making quite a racket while bumping into lights. They are found throughout the Palearctic region and, commonly seen from June to August, living in meadows, hedgerows, and gardens, and eating plants and tree foliage.</p>
